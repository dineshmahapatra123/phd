# The Torrens System

The Torrens System is a model of title registration in which the register is treated as the authoritative statement of ownership. It is usually contrasted with deeds registration, where the state records transactions but does not guarantee the validity of the underlying title.

## Relation to Existing Note
See full treatment: [[Torrens System]] (Wadhwa 1989, 2002). This note records the system's appearance in Pradeep Nayak's discussion of India's move from land reform to land titling.

## Why It Matters Here
The Torrens model matters because Indian land records remain largely presumptive. Policy arguments for conclusive titling often invoke Torrens principles, especially the idea that a public register should reduce transaction costs by making ownership transparent and secure.

## Caution for the Thesis
For Odisha and DILRMP, the key question is not whether Torrens is theoretically attractive. The question is whether the administrative preconditions exist: updated cadastral maps, reliable RoRs, low-dispute parcel histories, and institutional capacity to compensate or correct errors. Without those conditions, importing Torrens language may turn an uncertain record into an authoritative but still inaccurate record.
