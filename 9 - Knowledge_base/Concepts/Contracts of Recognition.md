# Contracts of Recognition

## Standard Definition
**Contracts of recognition** are Lund's term for the reciprocal relation through which claims become rights and institutions acquire authority. A claimant asks an institution to recognise a claim to land, membership, status, or entitlement. If the institution can successfully define and enforce that claim as a right, the claimant also recognises that institution's authority to do so.

The key point is reciprocal production: rights do not simply flow from a pre-existing state. The act of recognising rights also produces the authority of the recogniser. In Lund's formulation, authority and rights are tied together by recognition.

## Scholarly Debate
- **Lund (2016)**: Property, citizenship, and authority are mutually constituted. The state is not a finished object that grants rights; state quality emerges through successful recognition and enforcement of claims.
- **Northian NIE**: Treats the state largely as an enforcement mechanism that supplies property rights to reduce transaction costs. Lund shifts attention to how that enforcement mechanism itself is constituted through the politics of recognition.
- **Scott (1998)**: Emphasises legibility as the state's capacity to see and administer populations and land. Lund adds the reciprocal political layer: people seek visibility because recognition can transform claims into rights.

## PhD Application
This concept is directly useful for Chapters 3, 4, and 5. The Record of Rights is not merely a land record; it is a contract of recognition between the claimant and the revenue state. A name in the RoR signals that the state recognises a person as a landholding subject for mutation, compensation, welfare eligibility, taxation, and sometimes acquisition.

This helps sharpen the thesis argument on Odisha:

- **RoR as recognition**: Bhulekh does not just display data. It recognises a person as a rights-bearing land subject.
- **RoR as exclusion**: Tenants, sharecroppers, women with customary claims, and common-land users may be socially real but administratively invisible.
- **DILRMP as recursive authority**: By digitising and integrating RoR, cadastral maps, registration, ULPIN, and Aadhaar-linked identity, DILRMP strengthens the institutions that can recognise or deny property claims.

The framework is especially strong for the debate on welfare schemes such as PM-Kisan, CM-Kisan, and KALIA: a welfare claim becomes valid only when the state recognises the claimant through land-record categories.

## Measurement / Methods
- Compare RoR-recognised claimants with actual possessors, tenants, heirs, and customary users in the village survey.
- Trace which institution villagers approach for recognition: RI, Amin, Tehsildar, Sub-Registrar, court, panchayat, or informal broker.
- Record cases where a person has social recognition but lacks state recognition, or has state recognition without ground possession.
