# High Modernism

## Standard Definition
**High modernism** is Scott's term for the ideology that drives large-scale state simplification projects. It is "a strong, one might even say muscle-bound, version of the beliefs in scientific and technical progress... At its center was a supreme self-confidence about continued linear progress, the development of scientific and technical knowledge, the expansion of production, the rational design of social order, the growing satisfaction of human needs, and, not least, an increasing control over nature (including human nature) commensurate with scientific understanding of natural laws" (Scott, 1998, p. 4).

High modernism is not the exclusive property of the left or the right — it is the natural ideology of engineers, planners, technocrats, and high-level administrators. Scott notes that postcolonial elites have "marched under its banner" with particular energy, because it provides both a legitimating discourse and a set of instruments for rapid state-building.

High modernism is structurally dangerous not because its intentions are bad but because it **systematically delegitimises local knowledge** ([[Metis]]) and political contestation, and treats historical complexity as data quality problems to be engineered away.

## Three Conditions for High-Modernist Disaster (Scott's Formula)
1. **The desire** — provided by high-modernist ideology (technocratic confidence in scientific planning)
2. **The means** — provided by the modern state and its administrative machinery
3. **The levelled terrain** — provided by an incapacitated civil society unable to contest the plans

## Scholarly Debate
- **Scott (1998)**: High modernism is the ideological driver of schemes like Soviet collectivisation, Tanzanian villagisation, Haussmann's Paris, and Le Corbusier's urban planning. In each case, a technically coherent plan destroyed the complex, locally adaptive systems it replaced.
- **Scott's qualification**: Virtually every high-modernist intervention was undertaken with genuine popular support and has produced real benefits. The critique is structural, not moral. DILRMP is not simply wrong — it genuinely reduces the discretion of corrupt revenue officials and helps some marginalised landholders establish their rights. The danger is structural: the ideology underestimates complexity and suppresses the local knowledge that could correct its errors.

## PhD Application
DILRMP's intellectual register — *e-governance*, *transparency*, *ease of doing business*, *citizen-centric services* — is high-modernist in Scott's precise sense. The programme was designed by NIC engineers, implemented across party lines by UPA and NDA governments alike, and evaluated by NCAER, NIPFP, and IGIDR in purely technical efficiency terms. It is a technocratic project, not a partisan one.

**The radical break with history**: High modernism implies treating the structures of the past as products of "myth, superstition, and prejudice" that rational planning can correct. DILRMP's data-cleaning drive — resolving inconsistencies between RoR entries, maps, and registration records — treats the palimpsest of Indian land records as a data quality problem. The Mughal zabt categories, colonial settlement categories, and post-independence mutation records are not treated as socially meaningful historical sediment but as errors to be overwritten. This is the high-modernist move that your thesis must interrogate.

## Measurement / Methods
Identified through discourse analysis of programme design documents (DILRMP Guidelines 2008, 2021), evaluation reports (NCAER, NIPFP, IGIDR), and policy rhetoric. The convergence of NIE doctrine with Digital India ideology is its most visible manifestation in India.
