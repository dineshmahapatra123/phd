# Record of Rights (RoR)

The primary documentary mechanism used in India to record land ownership, tenancy, and revenue liabilities. Historically maintained by the colonial state for fiscal (revenue collection) rather than proprietary (ownership guarantee) purposes.

## Recognition Function
Lund (2016) allows the RoR to be read as more than a record. It is a state recognition instrument. A person named in the RoR is not only described as a landholder; they are recognised by the revenue state as someone whose claim can be acted upon for mutation, taxation, welfare eligibility, compensation, acquisition, and dispute processing.

This recognition function explains why errors in RoR matter beyond property. They affect citizenship-like access to state programmes. A tenant, woman heir, informal possessor, or commons user may have a socially valid claim but remain administratively invisible if the RoR does not recognise them.

## PhD Application
For Odisha, the RoR is the bridge between land governance and welfare governance. Bhulekh does not only digitise land entries; it digitises the state's recognition of landholding subjects. This directly supports the thesis argument on PM-Kisan, CM-Kisan, KALIA, and the exclusion of groups whose claims do not fit the record.
