# Territorialization and Land Records

## Standard Definition
**Territorialization** is the process by which political and legal techniques classify, map, register, and order space so that authority can regulate who belongs where and who may use which resources. Lund argues that territorialization does not merely structure physical space; it also organises the social and political perception of that space.

In land governance, territorialization joins two questions: what belongs to whom, and who belongs where.

## Scholarly Debate
- **Lund (2016)**: Spatial property and political territory are intertwined. Mapping, registration, and classification regulate land use, mobility, transactions, and citizenship.
- **Scott (1998)**: Cadastral mapping makes land legible for centralised administration. Lund adds that this legibility also structures belonging and membership.
- **Vandergeest and Peluso**: Territorialization is a state-power strategy that shapes access to resources by defining spaces and permissible uses.

## PhD Application
This concept is directly relevant to cadastral maps, Bhunaksha, ULPIN/Bhu-Aadhaar, and the spatial-textual disjuncture in Odisha. DILRMP's spatial layer is not simply a digital map. It is a territorialization project: it fixes parcel boundaries, attaches them to persons, and makes those relations actionable for mutation, taxation, welfare, land acquisition, and dispute resolution.

The Odisha problem is that territorialization may be spatially outdated. Where cadastral maps are based on old settlement operations, digitisation can strengthen a territorial order that no longer corresponds to possession, partition, land-use conversion, inheritance, or encroachment on the ground.

## Measurement / Methods
- Compare Bhunaksha/cadastral plot boundaries with physical possession measured during village fieldwork.
- Identify cases where textual RoR entries and spatial map boundaries recognise different realities.
- Record how villagers use plot number, khata number, boundary markers, and local memory to make territorial claims.
