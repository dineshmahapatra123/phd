# Property-Citizenship Nexus

## Standard Definition
The **property-citizenship nexus** is the analytical link between rights to valuable resources and recognised membership in a political community. Lund argues that property and citizenship are not separate domains: struggles over land are also struggles over who belongs, who is visible, and who can make valid claims on authority.

Property is a legitimised claim to something of value sanctioned by political authority. Citizenship is meaningful membership in an organised political body. Recognition is the shared element that makes both effective.

## Scholarly Debate
- **Lund (2016)**: Property and citizenship are co-produced through recognition. The ability to define who can hold land and who counts as a rights-bearing member is constitutive of state power.
- **Arendt/Somers tradition**: Citizenship is the "right to have rights." Lund applies this to land and resources: without recognised membership, property claims can be delegitimised.
- **NIE property-rights literature**: Often treats property as an economic institution for investment and exchange. Lund shows that property is also a political membership relation.

## PhD Application
This concept helps explain why land records in Odisha are not just technical instruments. A valid RoR entry can become a practical citizenship device: it determines who can access compensation, welfare transfers, agricultural schemes, institutional credit, and recognition in local disputes.

For the thesis, this is most relevant to:

- Chapter 3.3: RoR as a gate for PM-Kisan, CM-Kisan, and KALIA.
- Chapter 4.4: Bhulekh-based eligibility and exclusion in Odisha.
- Chapter 5: village-level gaps between socially recognised claims and state-recognised claims.

The crucial argument is that land-record modernisation may intensify citizenship stratification. People whose claims fit the RoR become administratively visible; those whose claims sit in tenancy, possession, inheritance disputes, women's customary rights, or commons use remain outside the state's recognised property-citizenship relation.

## Measurement / Methods
- Identify which welfare or compensation benefits require land-record recognition.
- Compare categories of village residents: recorded owners, possessors, tenants, women heirs, encroachers, commons users, and landless labourers.
- Track whether each category has access to state schemes mediated by land records.
