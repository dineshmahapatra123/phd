# Investment in Networks

## Standard Definition
Investment in networks is Berry's term for expenditures that strengthen a person's social identity, status, following, or bargaining position within the networks through which access to land, labour, capital, and state resources is negotiated.

These investments may include ceremonies, litigation, education of dependents, community contributions, patronage, support for kin, or other outlays that make claims more credible within a social field.

## Scholarly Debate
Berry challenges the assumption that surplus used outside direct production is simply inefficient consumption. In agrarian settings where access to resources depends on recognition, influence, testimony, kinship standing, or political connection, social expenditure can be a rational investment in the means of negotiation.

This also complicates NIE accounts of property-rights formalisation. If rights are partly maintained through social recognition and not merely legal registration, actors may invest in networks even when formal titling is available.

## PhD Application
For Odisha, this concept helps interpret why landholders and claimants may spend time and money on revenue intermediaries, caste networks, family negotiations, local political patrons, or litigation rather than only on formal mutation and record correction.

The concept is especially useful for:

- Chapter 5.6.2: experiences of mutation, partition, and correction.
- Chapter 5.7.2: revenue office interactions, intermediaries, and rent-seeking.
- Chapter 5.5.1: inheritance and partition claims.
- Chapter 5.8: welfare and land-record eligibility as arenas of claim-making.

## Measurement / Methods
Field indicators include:

- payments to intermediaries or witnesses in land disputes;
- reliance on local patrons for mutation or correction;
- kinship or caste mobilisation around partition and possession;
- expenditure on litigation or documentation to strengthen a contested claim;
- strategic maintenance of multiple social memberships to keep land claims open.
