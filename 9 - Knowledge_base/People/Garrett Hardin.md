# Garrett Hardin

Garrett Hardin is relevant as the author of the "tragedy of the commons" argument, a major reference point in debates over common property, open access, and the case for privatized or state-managed resource regimes.

## Scholarly Lens
Hardin's argument treats unregulated shared access as structurally prone to overuse: each user captures private benefit while spreading costs across the group. In property-rights debates, this became a powerful justification for assigning exclusive rights, strengthening state control, or converting common resources into private property.

## Critical Use in This Thesis
The PhD should use Hardin carefully. The useful point is the distinction between unmanaged open access and governed common property. Ostrom and related commons scholarship show that Hardin's model describes open access, not all communal tenure. This distinction matters for Indian land records because commons, customary rights, forest use, and village-level shared resources are often flattened or misclassified by formal record systems.
