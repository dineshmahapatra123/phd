# Mariano Sánchez-Talanquer

## Scholarly Lens
Mariano Sánchez-Talanquer studies state formation, territorial state capacity, and distributive conflict. His distinctive contribution to the land-records literature is to treat cadastral legibility as an arena that social actors strategically appropriate, rather than only a technology imposed by revenue-seeking rulers.

In the "one-eyed state" framework, landholders may demand registration when it secures legal recognition of ownership, while weakening valuation and taxation. State capacity is therefore multidimensional, geographically uneven, and shaped by conflicts among parties, landholders, officials, courts, and rival claimants.

## Core Bibliography
- Sánchez-Talanquer, M. (2020). "One-Eyed State: The politics of legibility and property taxation." *Latin American Politics and Society, 62*(3), 65–93.
- Sánchez-Talanquer, M. (2017). *States divided: History, conflict, and state formation in Mexico and Colombia* (doctoral dissertation).

## Inter-Person Links
- Extends [[James C. Scott]] by adding demand-side appropriation and elite capture to the legibility framework.
- Connects to [[Christian Lund]] because cadastral inscription recognises claims and simultaneously strengthens the authority of the recognising state.
- Challenges ruler-centred state-building accounts by showing that social groups can selectively build and deflect different components of state capacity.

## Methodological Bias
Sánchez-Talanquer combines comparative-historical institutional analysis, archival cadastral and fiscal records, qualitative historical evidence, and difference-in-differences estimation. The approach disaggregates state capacity by territory and function rather than treating it as a single national attribute.
