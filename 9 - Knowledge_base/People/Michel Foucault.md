# Michel Foucault

## Scholarly Lens
Michel Foucault (1926-1984) is useful for this thesis not as a general theorist of "power/knowledge," but specifically as a theorist of governmental rationality: the ways states and institutions make populations, conduct, economy, security, and welfare into objects of knowledge and intervention.

For the DILRMP thesis, the relevant Foucault is the Foucault of "Governmentality," not the broader cultural or literary use of Foucauldian discourse theory. His contribution is to show how modern rule operates through administrative knowledge, calculation, and the management of population rather than through sovereign command alone.

## Inter-Person Links
- **With [[James C. Scott]]**: Foucault provides the rationality of government; Scott provides the administrative mechanics of legibility, especially through cadastral maps and standardised records.
- **Against narrow NIE formalisation readings**: Foucault helps question whether land-record digitisation is only about efficiency, transaction costs, and secure property rights.
- **Useful alongside [[Bernard Cohn]]**: Cohn explains colonial knowledge practices in India; Foucault explains the broader governmental rationality through which knowledge becomes rule.

## Methodological Bias
Foucault works genealogically. He asks how particular forms of knowledge and intervention became possible, rather than whether a policy is simply true, false, efficient, or inefficient. Applied to DILRMP, this means asking how land becomes thinkable as a database object, a welfare filter, a market asset, a taxable parcel, and a governable administrative unit.
