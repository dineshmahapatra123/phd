# Christian Lund

## Scholarly Lens
Christian Lund is a political and institutional theorist of property, authority, and citizenship. His central contribution for this thesis is the argument that property rights and public authority are mutually constituted through recognition. Institutions do not merely enforce property rights; they become authoritative by successfully recognising, defining, and protecting claims as rights.

For the PhD, Lund is useful because he shifts the analysis of land records from "documents proving ownership" to "instruments through which the state recognises claimants and thereby reproduces authority."

## Inter-Person Links
- Extends the institutional pluralism tradition of [[Sally Falk Moore]] through the idea of semi-autonomous social fields.
- Complements [[James C. Scott]]: Scott explains state legibility; Lund explains the reciprocal politics of recognition.
- Provides a political corrective to [[Douglass North]] and NIE approaches that treat the state as an enforcement supplier rather than as something produced through claims and recognition.
- Connects to [[Bernard Cohn]] on colonial categorisation, but Lund's emphasis is less on knowledge production and more on rights-authority co-production.

## Methodological Bias
Lund favours grounded, historical, and institutional analysis over abstract state theory. He treats the state as an effect produced in concrete struggles over land, resources, membership, taxation, and jurisdiction. His method is especially useful for village-level land governance research because it asks which institution is actually recognised as authoritative in a given dispute.

## PhD Relevance
Lund's framework can anchor the thesis sections on RoR as welfare gate, recognition, and exclusion. It also helps interpret DILRMP as a recognition infrastructure: a digital system that strengthens the state's capacity to decide which claims count.
