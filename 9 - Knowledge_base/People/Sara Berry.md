# Sara Berry

## Scholarly Lens
Sara Berry reads agrarian change as a historical and political process shaped by negotiated access to resources. Her work resists both simple property-rights formalisation accounts and deterministic political-economy accounts by foregrounding ambiguity, social networks, authority, and contestation.

## Core Bibliography
- Berry, S. (1993). *No condition is permanent: The social dynamics of agrarian change in Sub-Saharan Africa*. University of Wisconsin Press.
- Berry, S. (2002). Debating the land question in Africa. *Comparative Studies in Society and History*, 44(4), 638-668.
- Berry, S. (2009). Property, authority and citizenship: Land claims, politics and the dynamics of social division in West Africa. *Development and Change*, 40(1), 23-45.

## Inter-Person Links
Berry is useful alongside [[Christian Lund]] because both treat property as a recognised and contested claim rather than as a settled object-right. She also challenges the formalisation logic associated with land titling and NIE policy debates.

## Methodological Bias
Berry's method is comparative historical political economy, grounded in agrarian history and close reading of land disputes, customary authority, market change, and social networks.

## PhD Relevance
Berry provides a conceptual bridge for Chapter 5: the Odisha fieldwork can treat RoR/possession discrepancies as active social negotiations rather than mere errors awaiting technical correction.
