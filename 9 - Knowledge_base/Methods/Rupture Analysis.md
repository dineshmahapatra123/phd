# Rupture Analysis

## Description
**Rupture analysis** is a qualitative and historical method that studies open moments when institutions, categories, and rights are unsettled. Lund uses rupture as an epistemological device: when authority is disrupted by colonialism, conflict, post-authoritarian transition, or major reform, the normally hidden process by which rights and authority are co-produced becomes visible.

Rupture analysis is related to trouble cases, diagnostic events, and situational analysis, but its specific value is that it reveals how new contracts of recognition are constructed.

## Strengths / Weaknesses
**Strengths**
- Makes visible the construction of authority that appears natural in stable periods.
- Useful for studying legal and institutional pluralism.
- Helps identify which categories of people and property are created, preserved, or excluded during reform.

**Weaknesses**
- Can overemphasise dramatic breaks and understate slow incremental change.
- Requires historical evidence to avoid treating every reform as a rupture.
- Non-Indian cases need careful translation before use in the Odisha thesis.

## PhD Relevance
The thesis can use rupture analysis for three moments:

1. **Colonial settlement**: the creation of revenue records and landholding categories.
2. **Post-independence reform**: abolition of intermediaries, tenancy reform, land ceilings, and Odisha-specific legal changes.
3. **Digital modernisation**: DILRMP, Bhulekh, Bhunaksha, ULPIN, and Aadhaar-linked land governance.

DILRMP can be read as a softer rupture: it does not abolish older categories, but it changes which claims become visible, searchable, linkable, and actionable by the state.
