Illegibly Legible

Outcomes of a Land Records Modernisation Programme in South India

Ramakumar & Ramesh Journal of Agrarian Change · 2023 Vol 23(4) · pp 729–754 TISS Mumbai

Paper at a Glance

What this paper does, who it's for, and why it matters.

🎯 Central Irony

Land records declared "purified" and made digitally legible by the state remained, in practice, illegible — inaccurate, contested, and politically loaded. The conceptual contribution behind the title "Illegibly Legible"

📍 Setting

Telangana's Land Records Updation Programme (LRUP), launched September 2017. It covered 10,283 villages, ~23.8 million acres, using 1,523 teams and 3,500 revenue officers. The fieldwork for this paper was conducted in Adilabad district in 2019.

🧵 Why It Was Really Done

The stated goal was to "purify" land records. The actual driver was the Rythu Bandhu Scheme (RBS) — a cash transfer of ₹5,000/acre/season requiring an accurate database of landowners — ahead of the 2018 state elections. LRUP served an electoral and welfare-targeting logic, not agrarian reform.

🔑 Theoretical Pillars

The paper draws on three interlocking frameworks:

  • Scott 1998  Seeing Like a State — legibility as state-simplification of complex realities
  • Polanyi 1944  Land as a "fictitious commodity" — eminently unsuited for market governance
  • Cole 2022  Technocracy — depoliticisation of governance through "instrumentally rational techniques"

💡 Core Contribution

The paper goes beyond Scott: neoliberal legibility-making doesn't simply simplify — it actively distorts in service of new power configurations (corporate land acquisition, welfare targeting, tenant exclusion). The "illegibly legible" concept captures digital transparency masking political opacity.

The Argumentative Chain

Follow the paper's logic step by step.

Premise 1 — Land is politically embedded

Following Marx and Polanyi, land is not a normal commodity. Its governance is necessarily political — tied to production, exploitation, and historical tenure. Any attempt to reduce it to a technical problem will fail.

Premise 2 — Colonial legibility created path dependence

Colonial cadastral surveys (Scott's legibility) codified land for revenue extraction — not for cultivators. In Telangana, the Nizam-era sethwar (1932–34) became the foundational document. Post-independence reforms layered onto this corrupted base without fixing it.

Premise 3 — Neoliberalism reoriented the project

From the 1980s, the World Bank and USAID promoted land records modernisation not for agrarian reform but for free land markets (EoDB, Torrens titling). India's DILRMP and Telangana's LRUP are products of this reorientation.

Observation — LRUP was driven by electoral logic

LRUP was rushed in 2017 to enable the Rythu Bandhu Scheme before elections. Its design prioritised speed and owner-identification over accuracy, equity, or genuine resurvey.

Finding — Five structural failures compounded

No fresh survey + political urgency + sethwar as infallible benchmark + deliberate tenant exclusion + corruption = a programme that reproduced and deepened illegibility under the guise of modernisation.

Conclusion — Technocracy cannot resolve political contradictions

"Illegibly legible": the Dharani portal created digital legibility that masked — and entrenched — political illegibility. The most vulnerable class (tenants, who actually cultivate) was rendered invisible in official records, losing credit, welfare, and legal recognition. Technocratic portals close off democratic accountability without resolving underlying land relations.

Five Structural Reasons for Failure

Click each card to expand the full explanation.

Failure A

The Conclusive vs. Presumptive Title Problem

A "curious third way" — neither here nor there.

No fresh cadastral resurvey was conducted. Only 3.6% of Telangana's area was resurveyed by April 2020. Yet the exercise went beyond simple presumptive updating — new titles derived from old, error-ridden presumptive titles. A bureaucrat explained resistance: "A cadastral survey would open a can of worms." The government's own 2022 sub-committee recommended a fresh digital field survey — effectively quashing its earlier claims that the database had become "authentic."
+
Failure B

The Political Urgency Problem

10 days per village instead of the needed 10 months.

The LRUP was hurriedly designed to enable a time-bound introduction of RBS. The 3-month timeline (extended by 3 more) was wholly inadequate for 10,000+ villages. Revenue functionaries collected data by day, performed data entry at night, slept in offices. An expert noted earlier pilots took six months for just eight villages. Officials told researchers: "Even with 10 months per village instead of 10 days, ground-level verification could not have been satisfactorily completed." The Revenue Department was also severely understaffed — Adilabad had 18 mandals but only 8–9 surveyors.
+
Failure C

The Sethwar Problem

An 85-year-old colonial document made infallible by software.

The Dharani portal was programmed to reject any data entry where area under new survey numbers differed from the 1932–34 sethwar by even one gunta. The sethwar bore little resemblance to ground reality after decades of partitions, transactions, manipulations, and encroachments. Cases that couldn't be reconciled were either resolved through the tehsildar's discretionary powers (itself a source of corruption) or dumped into the unresolved Part B. An estimated 200,000 cases were classified into Part B in haste, "just so that the tasks in the village could be completed on time."
+
Failure D

The Exclusion of Tenants

The most politically consequential outcome — deliberate erasure.

The Telangana government amended the 1971 AP Record of Rights Act in 2020 to remove the requirement to record tenants' interests. The Dharani portal required that owner and cultivator columns carry the same name — legally erasing tenancy. The Chief Minister stated: "Rythu Bandhu is for farmers. The government does not have details of tenant farmers." By the RSV's assessment, one-third of RBS payments went to absentee landlords. 60% of tenants reported lease rates rose after RBS. Tenants lost access to bank credit, crop insurance, PM Kisan, and legal recognition — violating the Land Licensed Cultivators Act 2011.
+
Failure E

Local Actor Subversion & Corruption

The portal became a site of opacity, not transparency.

Corruption was rampant. A tehsildar and VRO were arrested in 2019 for demanding bribes for corrections. Data entry was entrusted to contract workers with no stake in their tasks. Paradoxically, abolishing the patwari post (as an anti-corruption measure) eliminated the only official with genuine local land knowledge. The Dharani portal had no mechanisms for personal interaction, no accountability for complaint resolution, no legal immunity for collectors who acted on complaints. One collector sarcastically described being "reduced to Dharani Collectors." Human consequences included farmer suicides, death threats to officers, and a female MRO killed over a dispute.
+

🔗 Connecting Theme

All five failures share a root: the LRUP treated land illegibility as a technical problem of administrative dysfunction rather than a political condition produced by historical class struggle. Superimposing a digital portal on an unreformed political economy cannot resolve centuries of accumulated contradiction — it only closes off avenues of democratic redress.

Key Concepts & Terms

Theoretical vocabulary the paper uses and builds on.

Illegibly Legible
Ramakumar & Ramesh (2023)
Land records declared digitally transparent ("legible") by the state that remain, in practice, politically opaque, contested, and inaccurate. The concept captures how digital modernisation can mask rather than resolve underlying power relations. The paper's original theoretical contribution.
Legibility
Scott (1998)
The state's project of simplifying complex, locally managed social realities into standardised, administratively readable forms — enabling taxation, conscription, and control. Applied here to cadastral surveys that "translated" customary tenure for state revenue extraction.
Fictitious Commodity
Polanyi (1944)
Land, labour, and money are not produced for sale, yet capitalism treats them as commodities. Land is "eminently ill-suited to be a commodity" — its commodity description is "entirely fictitious." This explains why market-driven land titling schemes repeatedly fail.
Technocracy
Cole (2022)
"The administrative and political domination of society by a state elite … through the application of instrumentally rational techniques." Technocracy depoliticises governance, insulates state power from accountability, and fetishises the containment of political pressures. The Dharani portal is the paper's primary exhibit.
Path Dependence
Historical institutionalism
Colonial cadastral choices (the sethwar, the three-department structure, the RoR system) created lock-in effects that persisted for a century. Each reform layer was built atop a corrupted colonial foundation rather than replacing it.
Conclusive vs. Presumptive Title
Land admin literature
Conclusive title is state-guaranteed (Torrens system); presumptive title is rebuttable by evidence. The LRUP occupied a "curious third way" — neither guaranteeing titles nor simply recording presumptive ones, creating a system that appeared authoritative but carried neither standard's legal protections.
Rythu Bandhu Scheme (RBS)
Telangana, 2018
Direct income support of ₹5,000/acre/season to landowners (not tenants). Required accurate landowner database — the true motivation behind the LRUP. One-third of payments went to absentee landlords; tenants who actually cultivate received nothing and lost other entitlements.
Dharani Portal
Telangana state, 2020
The digital integrated land records system introduced post-LRUP. Programmed with the 1932–34 sethwar as infallible reference and required owner = cultivator in all records. Became a site of opacity — no personal interaction, no accountability for complaints, concentrated discretionary power.
Sethwar
Telangana, 1932–34
The colonial survey settlement document from the Nizam era, produced for revenue maximisation — not accurate tenure recording. Used as the Dharani portal's benchmark 85 years later. Its errors and omissions were effectively laundered into digital authority.
EoDB (Ease of Doing Business)
World Bank / neoliberal framework
The World Bank's framework that reoriented land records modernisation from agrarian reform toward facilitating investment and free land markets. By 2005, the Bank supervised land administration projects worth over $1 billion globally. The paper critiques how this agenda depoliticises land governance.

Historical Timeline

From colonial survey to the Dharani portal — a century of accumulated contradiction.

1932–34

Sethwar Survey Settlement

Nizam-era survey and bandobust operation produces the sethwar — benchmark revenue document, not a record of actual cultivation. This becomes the Dharani portal's reference document 85 years later.

1950

Hyderabad Tenancy & Agricultural Lands Act

Abolishes sarf khas and jagirdari systems. Introduces 38-E certificate for tenant protection. But the khasra pahani (1954) was prepared without fresh survey, using old sethwar — landlord pressure ensures patwaris list original holders, not actual cultivators. Records corrupted from the outset.

1973

Land Reforms (Ceiling on Agricultural Holdings) Act

Triggers wave of fraudulent manipulation: landlord benamis inserted in place of tenants, new survey numbers fabricated to evade ceiling laws. Revenue officials and patwaris complicit. "Most registers from the 1970s were products of manipulation."

1987

D.C. Wadhwa Committee

Documents scale of land records failure nationally. Recommendations not meaningfully implemented.

2002

Karnataka's Bhoomi

Digitisation of land records in Karnataka, promoted as anti-corruption success. Benjamin & Raman (2011) later argue it was designed to facilitate large business access to peri-urban land — treating illegibility as "normative" not "political."

2008

NLRMP (National Land Records Modernisation Programme)

National programme to modernise land records. Succeeded by DILRMP (2014). Impact assessments show 50% of Maharashtra sample parcels had >20% area deviation from records. Only 13% of Indian villages resurveyed by 2022.

Sep 2017

LRUP Launched — Telangana

Land Records Updation Programme launched under TRS government, ostensibly to "purify" records. Actual driver: imminent Rythu Bandhu Scheme. 1,523 teams, 3,500 revenue officers, 10-day target per village.

Jan 2018

Rythu Bandhu Scheme Launched

₹5,000/acre/season to landowners. One-third of payments go to absentee landlords. Tenants excluded. Lease rates rise for 60% of tenants surveyed.

2019

Fieldwork by Ramakumar & Ramesh (Adilabad)

Primary research conducted. 386 LRUP-related Kisan Mitra complaints analysed. Talamadagu village survey: no absentee landlord sharing RBS with tenants; not a single tenant recorded in land records.

2020

Dharani Portal Launched + Patwari Abolished

Integrated digital land records system. Simultaneously, patwari post abolished as anti-corruption measure — paradoxically eliminating the only official with local land knowledge. AP Record of Rights Act amended to remove tenant recording requirement.

2022

300,000+ Complaints Pending

State sub-committee acknowledges 20 types of problems with LRUP/Dharani. Recommends fresh digital field survey — implicitly quashing the government's earlier claims of 93% "purity." Hyderabad High Court chastises the government for non-responsiveness.
