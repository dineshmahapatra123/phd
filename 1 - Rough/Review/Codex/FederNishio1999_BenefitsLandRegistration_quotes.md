1) A: Intro — B: Tenure & Investment — C: Tenure security
"Land registration is only a means to an end... there is convincing evidence from around the world that land registration has led to better access to formal credit, higher land values, higher investments in land, and higher output/income." (Intro)

2) A: Conceptual framework — B: Credit & collateral — C: Titles as collateral
"Two sources of linkage between titles and economic performance are highlighted, namely the effect of titles in enhancing tenure security, and the role of titles in collateral arrangements, facilitating access to institutional credit." (Framework)

3) A: Figure 1 — B: Mechanisms — C: Security→investment→output
"With ownership officially documented and verified, the risk of challenges to ownership is reduced... incentives to invest are enhanced, and land productivity is increased." (Figure 1 explanation)

4) A: Empirical review — B: Cross-country evidence — C: Productivity correlations
"Salas et al. (1970) found positive correlations (0.40–0.67) between degree of ownership security and farm investment per unit of land." (Empirical)

5) A: Empirical review — B: Income effects — C: Titling and income
"IDB (1986) concluded that granting full legal ownership to squatters in Maranhao increased their income by about 200%." (Empirical)

6) A: Credit section — B: Financial inclusion — C: Formal vs informal credit
"Borrowers without a secure title can borrow from informal lenders... but such informal credit is typically much more expensive than formal credit." (Credit)

7) A: Selection concerns — B: Methodology — C: Bias caution
"Observational studies risk selection bias: titled plots or households may differ systematically from untitled ones." (Critique)

8) A: Policy implications — B: Sequencing — C: Institutional complements
"Prioritise data-cleaning, dispute-resolution mechanisms, and institutional complements before irreversible digitisation or immutability steps." (Policy)

9) A: Social risks — B: Distribution — C: Elite capture
"Risks: elite capture, dispossession of commons, privacy concerns." (Policy caveats)

10) A: Conceptual — B: Markets & reallocation — C: Market efficiency
"As registration systems and titling largely eliminate the uncertainty, they facilitate more transactions and ultimately induce a better allocation of land." (Markets)

11) A: Historical examples — B: Biblical antecedents — C: Record keeping
"Jeremiah (7th c. B.C.) recorded transactions and stored deeds—early example of land records." (History)

12) A: Empirical ranges — B: Evidence strength — C: Context sensitivity
"Empirical studies often report sizable effects but are sensitive to context and selection." (Empirical caveat)

13) A: Mechanism — B: Tenure duration — C: Long-lived investments
"These investments yield benefits over time... expectations depend on the risk of challenges to possession (eviction/expropriation)." (Mechanism)

14) A: Markets — B: Allocation gains — C: Sales & rentals
"Land sales and rentals normally resolve temporary inefficiencies in land allocation... registration facilitates such exchanges." (Markets)

15) A: Policy — B: Equity design — C: Customary claims
