## Axis A: By Thesis Chapter

### Ch. 1.2 (Literature context: property-rights orthodoxy)
1. "The developmental impact of institutions to establish and maintain secure property rights to land has been a subject of much debate."
   - **Relevance**: Opens the orthodox policy literature that treats land registration as a development tool.
   - **Page**: 233

2. "A key reason for the continued focus on property rights to land is the fact that in most economies land and real estate are a key part of households’ and firms’ wealth."
   - **Relevance**: Explains why titling is seen as a development lever.
   - **Page**: 234

---

### Ch. 2.4 (Legal and institutional infrastructure)
3. "Property rights are social conventions, backed by the enforcement power of the state (at various levels) or the community."
   - **Relevance**: Core definition of rights as institutional rather than natural.
   - **Page**: 235

4. "With ill-defined or incomplete property rights, those holding land need to spend resources to defend their rights."
   - **Relevance**: Shows why secure records matter in the orthodox theory.
   - **Page**: 236

5. "On the basis of well-defined land rights, low-cost access to reliable information on individuals’ landownership reduces transaction cost and uncertainty that hinder the exchange of land in markets for rental or sale and the use of land as collateral for credit."
   - **Relevance**: The classic registry argument.
   - **Page**: 238

---

### Ch. 3.1.2 (International titling orthodoxy)
6. "The notion that a lack of formal property rights to land may limit the scope for investment and development prompted bilateral and multilateral institutions to sponsor programs that would either systematically register land or improve the functioning of institutions of land administration."
   - **Relevance**: International agenda-setting around land titling.
   - **Page**: 234

7. "A simplistic and undifferentiated call for ‘formalization’ that lacks awareness of the complexity and long history of existing institutional arrangements is inappropriate and can make matters worse."
   - **Relevance**: The paper’s own major caveat against one-size-fits-all titling.
   - **Page**: 235

8. "Land registration may be viewed as an attempt to cement existing unequal arrangements rather than to overcome them."
   - **Relevance**: Crucial concession: formalisation can entrench inequality.
   - **Page**: 239

### Ch. 3.2.1 (Tenure security and state enforcement)
9. "Good governance—that is a consistent legal and institutional framework, broad access to information, and competent and impartial institutions (for example, courts and an honest bureaucracy) to enforce rights—is critical for making property rights effective."
   - **Relevance**: This is the paper’s key condition for success.
   - **Page**: 239

10. "Without institutions to enforce property rights effectively, the rights presumably protected by the land registry may exist only on paper and have little practical value."
    - **Relevance**: Strong support for the thesis critique of weak enforcement.
    - **Page**: 240

11. "A land registration system can be rendered ineffective by the absence of a judiciary that is accessible and impartial."
    - **Relevance**: Shows why titles fail in weak judicial environments.
    - **Page**: 240

---

### Ch. 3.2.3 (Costs and implementation)
12. "The cost of mapping increases exponentially with precision."
    - **Relevance**: Important warning against over-engineering cadastral systems.
    - **Page**: 245

13. "Cost of registering property is highly bimodal; while it is 2 percent or less of property value in 32 cases, it amounts to 5 or 10 percent and more of property values in 92 and 41 of the cases."
    - **Relevance**: Shows why transaction costs can remain high.
    - **Page**: 247

14. "To the extent that the costs exceed the benefits ... high cost can lead to a re-emergence of informal practices up to a point where ‘deformalization’ will undermine the sustainability of a land registry system."
    - **Relevance**: Critical for understanding why formal systems can decay.
    - **Page**: 247-248

---

### Ch. 3.4 (Technocratic limitations)
15. "Land registration has also been shown to increase activity in land rental markets, leading to higher efficiency overall. Evidence of improved access to credit, due to formalization of land rights, is scant."
    - **Relevance**: This is the paper’s own balanced summary of the evidence.
    - **Page**: 233

16. "Formalization of land rights should not be viewed as a panacea."
    - **Relevance**: Central caveat against triumphalist titling stories.
    - **Page**: 233

17. "The performance of land administration systems needs to be benchmarked in terms of coverage, cost-effectiveness, and quality of service provision."
    - **Relevance**: A useful diagnostic standard for evaluating DILRMP.
    - **Page**: 233

### Ch. 4.5 (Comparative implementation and governance)
18. "In such situations, it would be difficult to rely on state-issued documents and uncertainty of property rights, the elimination of which was the goal of setting up a registry system in the first place."
    - **Relevance**: Captures the vicious circle of weak governance and weak records.
    - **Page**: 240

19. "The disadvantages of an inadequate institutional framework can be exacerbated by the high cost of acquiring information on the legal regime or how rights can be enforced."
    - **Relevance**: Explains why information asymmetry deepens inequality.
    - **Page**: 240

### Ch. 5.5 (Ownership vs possession)
20. "Evidence shows that if first-time registration is introduced, unscrupulous individuals who are well connected and well informed may try to play the system."
    - **Relevance**: Shows how formalisation can be exploited by elites.
    - **Page**: 240

21. "Those holding land need to spend resources to defend their rights."
    - **Relevance**: Helps frame insecure possession as costly and socially wasteful.
    - **Page**: 236

---

### Ch. 5.8 (Credit, markets, and distribution)
22. "The use of collateral is a universal practice to reduce the extent of credit rationing and improve welfare."
    - **Relevance**: Represents the credit channel in the registration argument.
    - **Page**: 238

23. "In many instances, the ability to use land as collateral for credit may thus be limited to larger landowners."
    - **Relevance**: Important distributional caveat: credit benefits are skewed.
    - **Page**: 245

24. "While the benefits from land registration accrue to users, it will be desirable for registries to recover their cost through user fees."
    - **Relevance**: Highlights the fiscal logic and sustainability problem.
    - **Page**: 248
