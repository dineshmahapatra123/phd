2) A: II (Evolution) — B: Historical path dependence — C: British legacy
"Post-independence, India retained the British land records system, initially centred on revenue collection." (Evolution)

3) A: II — B: Path dependence — C: State variation

7) A: IV (Georgia) — B: Transferability — C: Long-run digitisation trajectory

8) A: V (Problematising) — B: Exclusion — C: Commons derecognition
"The DILRMP neglects the traditional tenure rights of marginalised farmers, tribal groups, and indigenous peoples... failing to recognise collective land claims in common lands." (Problematising)

9) A: V — B: Exclusion — C: Non-landowning farmers
"The digitisation of land records limits the definition of farmers to landowners, excluding those who work on leased land, engage in sharecropping, or are landless agricultural labourers." (Problematising)

10) A: V — B: Data quality — C: Garbage-in/garbage-out
"Gujarat halted creation of new records under DILRMP in 2018 after complaints... rectification necessitates a new survey of the land plot... raises the issue of potential data tampering before the data is put on a DLT-based platform." (Data quality)

11) A: V — B: Administrative frictions — C: Departmental silos
"Departments still work in silos and do not coordinate the updation of the data themselves, which risks discrepancies appearing later." (Data integration)

12) A: V — B: Judicial delays — C: Time-lag in dispute resolution
"NITI Aayog suggested that, on average, land-related disputes take about 20 years to be resolved in India." (Lacunas)

13) A: IV — B: Privacy — C: Data exposure risk

14) A: VI (Policy Roadmap) — B: Sequencing — C: Governance prerequisites

15) A: II — B: Distributional politics — C: Elite capture
"Government officials, as landowners, shared in gains from titling and were therefore willing to supply the institutional changes being demanded." (Evolution/Politics)

16) A: IV — B: Implementation risk — C: Backlog management
"Before reforming the recording and management system, not only do the encumbrances need to be addressed, but there is also a massive data backlog that needs clearing and uploading on a digital interface." (Operational risk)

17) A: III — B: Tech limits — C: DLT vs legality
"DLT can improve immutability and auditability but cannot substitute for legal reform, grievance redress, or legitimacy on the ground." (Synthesis)

18) A: V — B: Audit evidence — C: Tamil Nadu CAG findings
"CAG contended that digitisation improved access but the scheme’s outcomes were marred by significant deficiencies in converting manual records into digital records... 61% of villages studied had significant area differences." (Audit)

19) A: IV — B: International lessons — C: Sweden/Georgia/Ghana pilots
"Sweden's private blockchain pilot emphasised client-side verification and GDPR compliance; Georgia leveraged long-term digitisation to record 1.5m titles on blockchain." (International)

20) A: VI — B: Policy caution — C: Conditional adoption
"A DLT-based land titling and transaction system can track various land transactions and services, collaborating with financial institutions to update registries and enable smart transactions—but governance must precede scaling." (Roadmap)
