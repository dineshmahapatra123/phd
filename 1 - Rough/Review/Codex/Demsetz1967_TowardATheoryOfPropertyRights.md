## Core Argument

Demsetz argues that property rights are endogenous institutions that evolve to internalize externalities when the gains from internalization exceed the costs. Rights emerge when private benefits of exclusion or coordinated use justify the costs of defining, enforcing, and monitoring the right. The result is an efficiency-centred account: property rights appear where they lower social transaction costs and improve resource allocation.

---

## Conceptual Architecture

- Property rights as institutions: bundles of entitlements (use, transfer, exclusion) that structure incentives.
- Transaction-cost logic: the net benefit of defining a right must exceed enforcement costs for the right to be privately supported.
- Evolutionary/market-driven origins: rights evolve endogenously rather than being purely legal or moral abstractions.

---

## Empirical Terrain

Demsetz uses historical and comparative vignettes (pastoral/communal regimes, agricultural enclosure episodes) to show that changes in technology, market opportunities, or scarcity precipitate new property arrangements. The paper is conceptual and comparative rather than dataset-driven.

---

## Theoretical Lineage

Demsetz is a core reference in the New Institutional Economics (NIE) tradition: complements Coase on transaction costs and underpins later NIE treatments (North, de Soto, Deininger & Feder). His framing is frequently invoked by policy advocates who argue that formal titling and clear property rights reduce transaction costs and mobilise investment.

---

## Key Passages

- "When a transaction is concluded in the marketplace, two bundles of property rights are exchanged." (opening thesis)
- Property rights are justified when private benefits of internalization outweigh enforcement costs.

---

## Internal Tensions

- Distributional blind spot: Demsetz's efficiency focus sidesteps how rights creation can dispossess weaker claimants (commons users, customary holders).
- Power and politics: the market-based emergence of rights can reflect power asymmetries rather than neutral efficiency calculations.
- Applicability to India: the NIE mechanism underestimates administrative/institutional obstacles (mutation, registration) emphasised by Wadhwa.
