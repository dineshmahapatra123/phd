## Axis A: By Thesis Chapter

### Ch. 1.1 (Framework: What are institutions?)
1. "Institutions are the humanly devised constraints that structure human interaction and they exist to reduce the ubiquitous uncertainty arising from that interaction."
   - **Relevance**: Foundational definition. Institutions are not natural; they are designed to reduce uncertainty. DILRMP is an institutional innovation (digital records) attempting to reduce uncertainty about land ownership.
   - **Page**: 16

2. "How to bind the players to agreements across space and time. Game theory provides the (more or less trivial) solution: players can be bound when the gains from living up to agreements exceed the gains from defecting."
   - **Relevance**: Game theory is insufficient. Formal incentives alone don't create binding agreements. The deeper question is credibility.
   - **Page**: 11

3. "Institutions are the rules of the game and organizations are the players."
   - **Relevance**: Clarifies the level of analysis. Records are formal institutions; bureaucrats, farmers, courts are organizations that interpret and use them.
   - **Page**: 12

---

### Ch. 1.2 (Literature: How do property rights emerge?)
4. "In 1973...we made property rights the center of our explanation of economic performance. We slighted the complicated issues of enforcement and ignored the informal constraints of conventions and norms of behavior. Since then I have become convinced that the issue of the enforcement of property rights is central to credible commitment..."
   - **Relevance**: North's evolution: from property-rights focus (classical NIE) to enforcement-and-credibility focus (mature NIE). DILRMP follows the older, incomplete framework.
   - **Page**: 12

5. "Property rights are specified and enforced by polities and without an in depth understanding of the way polities evolve it is not possible to understand the way property rights evolve."
   - **Relevance**: Cannot understand land titling without understanding Indian state capacity and incentive structure. Polity matters.
   - **Page**: 13

### Ch. 3.2.1 (DILRMP within institutional framework: Why formal modernisation fails)
6. "There is, therefore, little evidence to support the view (apparently implicitly held by many economists doctoring the ailing economies of central and eastern Europe) that the necessary institutions will be the automatic outcome of getting the prices right through elimination of price and exchange controls."
   - **Relevance**: Directly critiques the logic behind many development programmes (including DILRMP): "modernise the formal rules and efficiency will follow." North argues this is naive.
   - **Page**: 12

7. "Throughout most of history and in much of the present world institutions have not provided the credible commitment necessary for the development of low-cost transacting in capital and other markets."
   - **Relevance**: Land markets in India have high transaction costs (title disputes, slow courts, bureaucratic delays). DILRMP assumes digitisation reduces these. But without credible commitment institutions, cost remains high.
   - **Page**: 11-12

---

### Ch. 4.2 (Mutation defects as institutional persistence)
8. "The institutional framework provides the incentives that dictate the kinds of skills and knowledge perceived to have the maximum pay-off...The organizations that come into existence will reflect the pay-off structure."
   - **Relevance**: Mutation bureaucracy is not incompetent; it reflects the incentive structure. If officials profit from mutation delays and corruption, they will continue despite digitisation. Digitisation doesn't change incentives.
   - **Page**: 17

9. "The result is a path dependent pattern in which the institutional matrix and the mental models of the players interact to shape incremental change."
   - **Relevance**: Mutation defects persist because they are embedded in path-dependent institutions (colonial record-keeping for extraction, postcolonial bureaucratic incentives for rent-seeking). Incremental formalisation won't eliminate them.
   - **Page**: 18

---

### Ch. 5.7 (Discrepancies between records and ground)
10. "Perceptions are derived from the mental constructs of the players."
   - **Relevance**: Why do records diverge from ground reality? Because officials' mental models shape how they interpret and execute record-keeping rules. If mental model is "records = extraction," they will use records for extraction.
   - **Page**: 17

## Axis B: By Active Debate

### Debate 1: Presumptive vs. Conclusive Titling
11. "A commitment is credible in either of two senses, the motivational or the imperative. A commitment is motivationally credible if the players continue to want to honor the commitment at the time of performance...It is credible in the imperative sense if the player cannot act otherwise because performance is coerced or discretion is dissabled."
   - **Relevance**: Presumptive titles rely on motivational credibility (state wants to honor); conclusive titles rely on imperative credibility (state cannot violate). India lacks both. Digitisation provides neither.
   - **Page**: 13

12. "Equally the constituents face the dilemma that the ruler may at some point renege on his promises and confiscate the accumulated wealth of his constituents."
   - **Relevance**: Farmers fear state confiscation or unfair expropriation. Even conclusive digital titles don't resolve this if the state retains de facto power to redefine "conclusive."
   - **Page**: 14

---

### Debate 3: Technocratic vs. Political Reform
13. "Successful restructuring of an economy entails a restructuring both of property rights to provide the 'correct' incentives and of the mental models of the players to induce choices that are complementary to such incentives."
   - **Relevance**: DILRMP focuses on property-rights restructuring (records). But mental-model restructuring (changing farmers' beliefs about state trustworthiness) is equally necessary and far harder. DILRMP does the former only.
   - **Page**: 22

14. "Restructuring property rights means not only creating the formal rules and creating and implementing a judicial system that will impartially enforce such rules."
   - **Relevance**: DILRMP creates new formal rules (digital records) but doesn't address the judicial system's impartiality. Without this, formal rules remain unenforced.
   - **Page**: 22

15. "It takes much longer to evolve norms of behavior than it does to create formal rules and for those economies without a heritage of such norms the reconstruction process is necessarily going to be long and the outcome very uncertain."
   - **Relevance**: DILRMP is a formal rule (can be created in 5 years). Norms of state trustworthiness (can take 50+ years to evolve). Mismatch between programme timeline and institutional reality.
   - **Page**: 22

---

### Debate 4: Colonial-to-Postcolonial Continuity
16. "The mental models of the actors (as reflected in the intellectual tradition of Hobbes, Locke, Montesquieu, and the Scottish enlightenment) were equally context specific."
   - **Relevance**: Colonial mental models (records = extraction, state = predatory) were context-specific. Postcolonial mental models inherited the same context. DILRMP cannot simply impose new mental models.
   - **Page**: 16-17

17. "The interaction between the mental models the members of a society possess and the institutional structure and consequent organizations is the key to path dependence. It is not only...that the entrepreneurs of the existing organizations have a stake in the existing institutional structure, but it is also that the mental models that have evolved with that institutional structure will be broadly complementary to the existing institutional framework."
   - **Relevance**: Colonial path-dependence: records created for extraction; mental models co-evolved with extraction logic. Postcolonial bureaucrats have mental models aligned with this extractive institutional context. DILRMP doesn't break this path.
   - **Page**: 20

---

### Debate 7: NIE vs. Social Relations
18. "Human beings are—to use Simon's phrase—intendedly rational. But the complexity of the problems to be solved and the limitations of the mental models that humans construct to solve them have produced throughout history a vastly different story than would be human history in a world populated by individuals in possession of the rationality implied by economic theory."
   - **Relevance**: Critiques neoclassical rationality. Land decisions are complex; farmers use mental models, not rational calculation. DILRMP assumes farmers will rationally calculate land-titling benefits; instead, they rely on ideology and experience.
   - **Page**: 15

## Axis C: By Core Concept

### Concept: Credible Commitment & Enforcement
19. "It is at this point that both types of credible commitment enter the picture. The ruler may be able to structure the game so that it is both in his and his constituents' interest to abide by the rules—motivational credibility. Or the ruler may find that further wealth accumulation can only be realized by binding himself irreversibly (such as giving over rights and coercive power to constituents or their representatives)—imperative credibility."
   - **Relevance**: DILRMP assumes motivational credibility (state will want to honor titles because it profits from market growth). But India's institutional history suggests low motivational credibility. Imperative credibility (courts binding state) is weak (slow, corrupt).
   - **Page**: 14

---

### Concept: Path Dependence & Institutional Lock-in
20. "The economies of scope, complementarities, and network externalities of an institutional matrix make institutional change overwhelmingly incremental and path dependent."
   - **Relevance**: Land-record systems have path dependence; extractive practices complement extraction-incentives; bureaucrats have invested skills in extraction. DILRMP's digitisation is formally innovative but incrementally embedded within extractive logic.
   - **Page**: 17

---

### Concept: Formal-Informal Alignment
21. "Formal rules are an important part of the institutional framework but only a part. To work effectively they must be complemented by informal constraints (conventions, norms of behavior) that supplement them and reduce enforcement costs. If the formal rules and informal constraints are inconsistent with each other the resulting tension is going to induce political instability."
   - **Relevance**: DILRMP's formal rules (digital records, standardised procedures) may conflict with informal norms (records = extraction, bureaucrats = predatory). This tension will destabilise the system.
   - **Page**: 20

---

### Concept: Ideas, Ideologies, Mental Models
22. "The crucial issues that determine the long run performance of economies and polities are complex, subject to contradictory theories that cannot be resolved with the information available even if the constituent did have the incentive to be informed. Ideological stereotypes take over and provide the basis for choices in such context."
   - **Relevance**: DILRMP's claims about records enabling growth are theoretically contested and empirically unresolvable at farmer level. Instead, ideology dominates: "government records are extraction" (farmer ideology) vs. "records enable markets" (technocrat ideology).
   - **Page**: 19
