# Chapter 3 Reading Architecture

**Status:** Finalisation architecture, fully refreshed and cross-checked June 2026  
**Purpose:** Reading, note-making and drafting plan, not a bibliography to cite exhaustively

## Political Economy of Land Records Modernisation in India

This architecture follows Chapter 3 in `Chapters_Sections.pdf` and the research problem in
`Proposal_Dinesh_Mahapatra.pdf`. Chapter 2 reconstructed the historical and institutional
genealogy of land records. Chapter 3 must explain the contemporary policy project: why
modernisation acquired urgency, which development claims justify it, how DILRMP seeks to
reorganise land information, and how this infrastructure serves both land markets and the
welfare state.

The chapter should not begin from the conclusion that DILRMP is either emancipatory or
dispossessory. It should reconstruct and evaluate rival causal propositions.

## The Argument Chapter 3 Should Build

The chapter can advance the following argument:

1. Since the late twentieth century, Indian land policy has moved from an overriding
   emphasis on redistributive agrarian reform toward a broader agenda of transferable
   property, functioning land markets, infrastructure acquisition, investment facilitation and
   targeted welfare.
2. This is not a complete replacement of the postcolonial developmental state by a
   neoliberal state. Redistributive laws, protective restrictions, fiscal administration,
   market-making and welfare targeting coexist within a layered institutional order.
3. International institutions, especially the World Bank, helped consolidate a policy
   vocabulary linking secure and transferable land rights to investment, credit, efficient
   allocation, poverty reduction and good governance. This vocabulary evolved over time and
   should not be reduced to a single de Soto-style doctrine.
4. DILRMP is an institutional programme for making land information comprehensive,
   interoperable, spatially precise, searchable and continuously updateable. These qualities
   potentially reduce transaction costs and administrative discretion, but they also determine
   which claims can circulate through markets and state databases.
5. Integration is not the same as accuracy. Connecting RoRs, cadastral maps, registration,
   courts and identification systems may propagate legacy errors unless rights are
   substantively adjudicated and records are reconciled with possession.
6. The movement toward conclusive title transfers risk among the state, buyers, recorded
   owners, unrecorded claimants and the public treasury. It is therefore a distributive and
   constitutional project, not merely a superior recording technique.
7. Land records now operate as welfare infrastructure. When eligibility is derived from
   recorded ownership, the state converts an administrative record into a gatekeeping device
   that can include recorded landholders while excluding tenants, sharecroppers, women,
   heirs, forest users and cultivators whose claims are absent or outdated.
8. Technocratic reform depoliticises land when conflicts over ownership, caste, gender,
   tenancy and commons are represented as problems of data quality, interoperability or
   service delivery alone. Technology can nevertheless create real gains; the question is
   which problems it can solve, which it displaces, and whose claims it recognises.

The chapter should end with propositions that Chapters 4-6 test in Odisha.

## Direct Relationship to the Thesis Questions

Chapter 3 should provide the national-level explanations and evaluative criteria needed for
the proposal's questions about:

1. The motivations, objectives and design of land-records modernisation.
2. Whether programme design is consistent with claimed outcomes.
3. How RoRs enable land transfer, acquisition and welfare access for different groups.
4. Why textual records, cadastral maps, ownership and possession diverge.
5. Whether modernisation is technocratic in its diagnosis and implementation.
6. Whether integration, online updating, ULPIN and technology-assisted survey can substitute
   for, or only supplement, substantive survey, settlement and adjudication.

This chapter should formulate the mechanisms. Chapters 4-6 should supply the Odisha,
village and parcel-level evidence.

## Analytical Framework: Four Logics in Tension

### 1. Efficiency and transaction-cost logic

Accurate records and enforceable rights are expected to reduce information asymmetry,
search costs, fraud, disputes and the cost of transfer and mortgage. This is the NIE and
mainstream development-policy case.

### 2. Accumulation and market-making logic

Formal, transferable and spatially precise rights make land easier to acquire, consolidate,
mortgage, value and repurpose. The state creates the legal and informational infrastructure
for land markets; resulting gains and losses depend on class, caste, gender and bargaining
power.

### 3. Administrative and welfare logic

Integrated records allow the state to tax, regulate, acquire, plan, identify beneficiaries,
transfer cash and monitor compliance. The same record may therefore function as market
infrastructure and welfare infrastructure.

### 4. Recognition and exclusion logic

Digital systems recognise claims that can be standardised and verified. Socially legitimate
but unrecorded claims may become less visible when the digital record is treated as the
authoritative reality.

These are not mutually exclusive. A strong chapter shows how they coexist and conflict.

## Cross-Cutting Institutional Sequence

Trace each reform through the same sequence:

`claim -> inscription in a record -> verification -> integration -> administrative decision ->
material consequence -> correction or appeal`

This prevents the chapter from treating "digitisation" as one event. At each stage, ask:

- Who can initiate or contest the process?
- Which document, database or official has authority?
- What happens to claims that cannot be standardised?
- Does an error remain local, or propagate across integrated systems?
- Is correction cheaper and more accessible than the benefit or transaction at stake?

## Evidence Discipline

Do not treat the following as interchangeable evidence:

| Evidence | What it can establish | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Programme guidelines | Intended design and official causal theory | Implementation or outcomes |
| MIS completion figures | Recorded administrative coverage | Accuracy, currency or legal validity |
| Portal availability | Digital access in principle | Effective access or successful correction |
| Statutes and judgments | Formal legal authority | Routine administrative practice |
| Audit and impact reports | Selected implementation failures or effects | Universal state-level conditions |
| Interviews and process tracing | Mechanisms, discretion and institutional practice | Population prevalence without sampling |
| Household and parcel comparison | Record-possession-map correspondence in study sites | National representativeness |

## Priority Key

- **A - Essential:** read closely and use in the chapter's main argument.
- **B - Important:** read selectively for a subsection, comparison or counterargument.
- **C - Reference:** consult for factual verification or additional cases.
- **Primary:** legislation, policy guidelines, committee report, audit or administrative data.

### Drafting move

Define neoliberal land governance as a reorientation of state action toward making rights
transferable, land markets operable, information legible and investment risks calculable.
Do not define it as state withdrawal. The modernising state is active: it surveys, adjudicates,
digitises, integrates, identifies, compensates, acquires and guarantees.

### 3.1.1 Transition from Agrarian to Market-Oriented Reforms

The subsection should distinguish four policy orientations:

1. Abolition of intermediaries, tenancy reform, ceilings and redistribution.
2. Green Revolution-era productivity and agrarian differentiation.
3. Liberalisation, leasing reform, land acquisition, infrastructure and urban expansion.
4. Record modernisation as the informational infrastructure of markets and welfare.

### Analytical cautions

- Do not claim the World Bank mechanically caused DILRMP without documentary evidence.
- Distinguish intellectual influence, financing, policy diffusion, technical expertise,
  benchmarking and direct programme participation.
- The Bank's policy has evolved and contains internal qualifications about governance,
  gender, commons, adjudication and context.
- Identify which Bank propositions entered Indian policy documents and which did not.
- Keep **market-led agrarian reform**, **land administration reform** and **digital
  government reform** analytically separate. They overlap, but they are not one policy.

### Drafting move

Reconstruct a policy chain:

`secure rights -> investment incentives -> credit/collateral -> transfers to efficient users ->
growth and poverty reduction`

Then evaluate every arrow separately. The question is not whether institutions matter, but
which institutions, under what social relations, and with what distributive effects.

### 3.2.1 The Design of DILRMP

### Primary sources

1. **Primary, A** Government of India, Department of Land Resources. 2008-09.
   *National Land Records Modernization Programme: Guidelines, Technical Manuals
   and MIS*.
   - Read in full. Extract objectives, components, funding, institutional responsibilities,
     sequencing and the relationship to conclusive title.

2. **Primary, A** Department of Land Resources. Current *Digital India Land Records
   Modernization Programme* guidelines, MIS definitions, annual reports, parliamentary
   replies and component documents.
   - Current components include RoR computerisation, cadastral-map digitisation and
     georeferencing, integration, registration computerisation, modern record rooms,
     revenue-court management and voluntary Aadhaar linkage.
   - Official page: https://dolr.gov.in/programmes-schemes/dilrmp-2/
   - Live MIS: https://dilrmp.gov.in/
   - Use the live MIS 4.0, launched on 15 October 2024, but archive every progress table used
     because dashboard values and definitions can change.
   - A November 2024 official statement described DILRMP as extended through 2025-26.
     Since that period has ended, verify the post-March 2026 financial and administrative
     status before making claims about the programme's current phase.

3. **Primary, A** Department of Land Resources documents on:
   - ULPIN/Bhu-Aadhaar.
   - National Generic Document Registration System (NGDRS).
   - e-Registration and SRO-tehsil integration.
   - Computerisation of Revenue Court Management System.
   - Linkage of e-Courts with land-record and registration databases.
   - Transliteration and multilingual RoRs.

4. **Primary, B** Parliamentary Standing Committee reports and CAG performance audits
   concerning DILRMP, registration, survey/resurvey and state land-record management.

5. **Primary, B** Survey of Villages and Mapping with Improvised Technology in Village
   Areas (SVAMITVA) guidelines and implementation material.
   - Use only as a boundary/comparator: SVAMITVA concerns inhabited village abadi areas and
     property cards, while DILRMP has a different institutional scope and record genealogy.

### Terminology that must remain separate

- **ULPIN/Bhu-Aadhaar:** a parcel identifier derived from geospatial coordinates.
- **Aadhaar seeding/linkage:** linking a person identifier to land-record data.
- **Georeferencing:** locating cadastral information in a coordinate reference system.
- **Resurvey:** measuring or re-establishing parcel geometry and boundaries.
- **Integration:** enabling data exchange or workflow across systems.

None of these operations, by itself, adjudicates ownership.

### Core analytical questions

- Does the programme digitise existing information or create/adjudicate rights?
- Which components improve access, which improve accuracy, and which improve
  interoperability?
- Is completion measured by coverage, service availability, legal validity or ground truth?
- What is centrally standardised and what remains within state law and administration?
- What grievance and correction mechanisms accompany digitisation?
- Does programme sequencing require updating before integration? Is this implemented?
- What capacities are assumed at tehsil, RI, survey, registration and court levels?

### Drafting move

Explain DILRMP as a **federated integration project** operating in a constitutionally
decentralised field. Its ambition is national interoperability, but its data are produced under
heterogeneous state laws, terminologies, survey histories and administrative capacities.

### 3.2.2 Integration of RoR, Cadastral Maps and Registration

Integration should be separated into four distinct operations:

1. **Identity integration:** linking persons and parcels.
2. **Spatial-textual integration:** linking RoR entries to mapped parcels.
3. **Transactional integration:** linking registered deeds to mutation and updated RoRs.
4. **Institutional integration:** connecting revenue, survey, registration and courts.

### Essential readings

1. **A** Zevenbergen, Jaap. 2002. *Systems of Land Registration: Aspects and Effects*.
   Netherlands Geodetic Commission.
   - Conceptual standard for legal and organisational integration.

2. **A** Goswami et al. 2021. *Land Records Modernisation in India*.
   - Use its institutional interfaces and state cases.

3. **A** Ramakumar and Ramesh 2023, "Illegibly Legible."
   - Direct warning that integration can stabilise data without resolving its relation to actual
     possession and rights.

4. **A** NCAER/IGIDR/NIPFP 2017 pilot impact assessment.
   - Its real-time mirror method is especially relevant to the integration claim.

5. **B** Rao, S. S., et al. 2014. "Assessing Usefulness of High-Resolution Satellite
   Imagery for Re-Survey of Cadastral Maps." *ISPRS Annals* II-8: 133-143.
   DOI: 10.5194/isprsannals-II-8-133-2014.
   - Technical possibilities and limitations of imagery-based cadastral resurvey.

6. **B** Kaushik, Anjali. 2020. "New Technology Interventions Including Blockchain
   Technology in Land Record and Registry Management in India." In *ICEGOV 2020*.
   DOI: 10.1145/3428502.3428521.
   - Useful as a statement of technology-led reform; evaluate its governance assumptions.

7. **B** World Bank. 2017. *India Land Governance Assessment*.
   - National diagnosis of missing authoritative textual and spatial records.

8. **B** Enemark, Stig, Keith Clifford Bell, Christiaan Lemmen, and Robin McLaren.
   2014. *Fit-for-Purpose Land Administration*. FIG and World Bank.
   - Read as the principal pragmatic case for scalable, flexible and incrementally improvable
     spatial frameworks; assess what "fit" means and who defines the purpose.

### Primary sources

9. **Primary, A** Registration Act, 1908; state land-revenue laws and mutation rules;
   survey and settlement laws; relevant registration-mutation integration orders.

10. **Primary, A** DILRMP MIS metadata and definitions.
   - Verify what "integration completed" means before using progress percentages.

11. **Primary, B** Department of Land Resources, e-Court linkage and ULPIN technical
    documentation.

12. **C** ISO 19152, *Land Administration Domain Model*.
    - Consult only for the information-model and interoperability vocabulary; it is not a
      theory of socially legitimate land rights.

### Integration failures to distinguish

- Correct person, wrong parcel.
- Correct parcel, outdated owner.
- Registered transfer without mutation.
- Mutation without map subdivision.
- Joint RoR masking internal possession.
- Cadastral boundary inconsistent with occupation.
- Court stay/decree absent from transactional search.
- Same person represented differently across databases.
- Digitally identical records that remain legally inconsistent.

### Analytical proposition

Interoperability reduces the cost of accessing and comparing state-held information. It does
not itself establish that the information is true, complete or just. Integration can make both
accurate and inaccurate records more consequential.

### Challenges to organise

1. **First registration:** How are existing claims conclusively adjudicated?
2. **Notice and participation:** Who receives notice, and can weaker claimants contest?
3. **Boundary certainty:** Can title be guaranteed where cadastral maps are outdated?
4. **Layered interests:** How are tenants, women, heirs, easements, commons and customary
   rights represented?
5. **Institutional competence:** Does an administrative registrar perform quasi-judicial
   functions?
6. **Fraud and corruption:** Does conclusiveness protect a fraudulent transferee or the
   dispossessed prior owner?
7. **Indemnity:** Who finances compensation, and is compensation an adequate remedy?
8. **Appeal and finality:** How are curtain/finality principles reconciled with natural justice?
9. **Transition costs:** How are millions of legacy parcels investigated and insured?
10. **Federal diversity:** Can a model law accommodate different state revenue systems?

### Risk-allocation matrix

| System | Buyer bears | Recorded owner bears | Unrecorded claimant bears | State bears |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Presumptive/deeds-based | | | | |
| Conclusive title without indemnity | | | | |
| Torrens-style title with indemnity | | | | |
| Title insurance model | | | | |

The key question is not whether certainty is desirable, but who pays for certainty and whose
claims are extinguished to produce it.

## 3.3 State Welfare: Role of Land Records in Direct Benefit Transfer Systems

This section is not a general review of DBT. Its object is the conversion of land records into
eligibility infrastructure.

### Central argument

Linking welfare to land records has two opposing effects:

- It can provide an administratively cheap, auditable and rule-bound way to identify
  beneficiaries.
- It can equate "farmer" with "recorded landholder," excluding actual cultivators and
  reproducing historical inequalities contained in the record.

### Conceptual and welfare-state readings

1. **A** Mkandawire, Thandika. 2005. *Targeting and Universalism in Poverty
   Reduction*. UNRISD Social Policy and Development Programme Paper No. 23.
   - Essential political economy of targeting, administrative costs and social solidarity.

2. **A** Ferguson, James. 2015. *Give a Man a Fish: Reflections on the New Politics of
   Distribution*. Duke University Press.
   - Cash transfers and distributive politics beyond employment-centred development.

3. **A** Khera, Reetika. 2017. "Impact of Aadhaar on Welfare Programmes."
   *Economic and Political Weekly* 52 (50).
   - Core India evidence on authentication, mandatory linkage and exclusion.

4. **A** Masiero, Silvia, and Soumyo Das. 2019. "Datafying Anti-Poverty Programmes:
   Implications for Data Justice." *Information, Communication & Society* 22 (7):
   916-933. DOI: 10.1080/1369118X.2019.1575448.
   - Direct framework for analysing databases as distributive institutions.

5. **B** Khera, Reetika, ed. 2019. *Dissent on Aadhaar: Big Data Meets Big Brother*.
   Orient BlackSwan.
   - Broader legal, empirical and political critique of digital identity infrastructure.

6. **B** Eubanks, Virginia. 2018. *Automating Inequality: How High-Tech Tools Profile,
   Police, and Punish the Poor*. St. Martin's Press.
   - Comparative framework for automated eligibility and feedback loops. Use comparatively,

7. **B** Jain, Charu, Disha Saxena, Somnath Sen, and Deepak Sanan. 2023. "Women's

   Rights and Freedoms Globally." *Big Data & Society* 4 (2).
   - Provides a framework for visibility, representation and non-discrimination in data
     infrastructures.

### PM-KISAN and land-record sources

9. **Primary, A** Government of India, Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare.
   *PM-KISAN Operational Guidelines*, revised guidelines, FAQs, clarifications and
   Aadhaar-seeding instructions.
   - Read the eligibility definition, cut-off date, succession provisions, ownership changes,

   - Official guidelines: https://pmkisan.gov.in/Documents/RevisedPM-KISANOperationalGuidelines%28English%29.pdf

### From PM-KISAN to Farmer Registry and AgriStack

The section must now extend beyond PM-KISAN. The Digital Agriculture Mission, approved

### Registry questions to answer

- Does the system identify owners, cultivators, claimants, or different categories?
- What event updates the Farmer Registry: mutation, crop survey, self-declaration or field
  verification?
- Can a person challenge a wrong person-parcel link without first correcting the RoR?
- Does dynamic linkage reduce repeated paperwork or spread a legacy error more rapidly?
- Which public and private actors may query or receive land-linked farmer data?
- Can non-registration become a practical barrier even where a scheme does not legally make
  Farmer ID mandatory?
- What happens to oral tenants, sharecroppers and women cultivating jointly held land?

### Beneficiary categories to analyse

| Social position | Cultivates? | Recorded owner? | Typical DBT position |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Owner-cultivator | Yes | Yes | Most legible |
| Tenant/sharecropper | Yes | No | Structurally vulnerable to exclusion |
| Woman cultivator/heir | Often | Jointly/absent | Dependent on inheritance and mutation |
| Landless agricultural worker | Yes, as labour | No | Outside landholder-based scheme |
| Absentee owner | No | Yes | May remain legible subject to exclusions |
| Forest-right claimant/title holder | Yes/uses land | Variable | Depends on record integration |
| Encroacher/regularisation claimant | Yes | No/contested | Usually illegible until formalised |
| Multi-owner family member | Variable | Joint entry | Eligibility and family-unit ambiguity |

### Mechanisms of exclusion and error

- Outdated inheritance and partition.
- Oral tenancy and concealed leasing.
- Gendered non-recording of inherited rights.
- Name, parcel, bank and Aadhaar mismatches.
- Fixed cut-off dates interacting with later transfers.
- Non-digitised or non-integrated records.
- Multiple databases defining household/family differently.
- Dynamic propagation of a wrong land-person link into Farmer Registry and connected
  services.
- Weak notice, correction and grievance systems.
- Treating absence from records as absence of entitlement.

### Scope boundary

Chapter 3 should establish the national design and analytical problem. Detailed CM-KISAN,
PM-KISAN and Odisha evidence belongs in Chapters 4 and 5.

## 3.4 Critical Reflections on Technocratic Reforms

Technocracy should be defined precisely: the conversion of politically contested questions
into problems represented as solvable through expert knowledge, administrative procedure,
metrics and technology.

     while expanding bureaucratic power.

   University of California Press.

   Classification and Its Consequences*. MIT Press.
   - Classification systems embed values and create residual categories.

6. **B** Winner, Langdon. 1980. "Do Artifacts Have Politics?" *Daedalus* 109 (1):
   121-136.
   - A concise way to reject technological neutrality.

7. **B** Jasanoff, Sheila, ed. 2004. *States of Knowledge: The Co-Production of Science

    - Prevents an abstract "technology versus society" framing by examining everyday state

### A technology-capability matrix

| Problem | Technology can plausibly improve | Technology cannot resolve alone |
|---|---|---|
| Slow retrieval | Search and online access | Whether the underlying right is valid |
| Record tampering | Logs and access controls | Collusion before data entry |
| Deed-mutation delay | Workflow integration | Contested inheritance or tenancy |
| Map deterioration | Scanning/georeferencing | Socially disputed boundaries |
| Duplicate identities | Standardised identifiers | Legitimate layered/shared claims |
| Welfare leakage | Database matching/audit | Normative definition of "farmer" |
| Repeated verification | Reusable Farmer ID and linked records | Fair inclusion of tenants and unrecorded cultivators |
| Disputes | Information and case tracking | Adjudication, power and affordability |
| Corruption | Reduce some discretion | Shift corruption to correction/data entry |

### Evaluate technocracy through seven tests

1. **Problem diagnosis:** Is a political/legal problem misidentified as an information
   problem?
2. **Data provenance:** How was the analogue record created, and whose claims were absent?
3. **Correction:** Can affected people challenge and change the digital record?
4. **Distribution:** Who gains from lower transaction costs and who bears error costs?
5. **Accountability:** Who is answerable when integrated systems produce wrongful outcomes?
6. **Function creep:** Is data collected for land administration reused for welfare, credit,
   insurance, acquisition or commercial profiling without adequate safeguards?
7. **Mandatory effects:** Is a formally optional identifier becoming practically necessary to
   transact with the state?

### Avoid two symmetrical errors

- **Technological solutionism:** assuming digitisation necessarily creates accurate,
  corruption-free and inclusive governance.
- **Technological fatalism:** assuming every digital intervention necessarily worsens
  dispossession or exclusion.

The empirical task is to identify mechanisms and conditions.

## Propositions to Carry into Chapters 4-6

1. Digitisation improves access faster than it improves substantive accuracy.
2. Integration reduces administrative friction but may magnify legacy errors.
3. Households with simple, current, single-owner records benefit more readily than
   households with inheritance, partition, tenancy or possession conflicts.
4. Record-linked welfare privileges recorded ownership over actual cultivation.
5. Women and tenants face distinctive exclusion mechanisms, not merely generic digital
   divides.
6. Lower discretion in document delivery may coexist with discretion in mutation,
   correction, survey and grievance redressal.
7. Modernisation facilitates multiple state projects simultaneously: service delivery,
   taxation, acquisition, markets, welfare and surveillance.
8. The effect of technology depends on the prior distribution of rights, administrative
   capacity and opportunities for contestation.
9. Dynamic linkage can reduce repeated verification while also allowing a single land-record
   error to affect multiple schemes and services.
10. Farmer Registry will remain ownership-centred unless states create credible procedures
    for recognising tenants, lessees and other cultivators.

These are hypotheses or sensitising propositions, not findings to announce before fieldwork.
