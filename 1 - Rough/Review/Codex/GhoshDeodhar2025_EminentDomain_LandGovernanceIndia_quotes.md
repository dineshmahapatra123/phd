3) A: Colonial drivers — B: Political economy — C: Opium & tea
"British land policies enabled the forced cultivation of opium and establishment of tea plantations to correct Britain’s trade imbalance with China and sustain global dominance." (Intro)

4) A: Santhal uprising — B: Indigenous resistance — C: Communal land protection
"The Santhal rebellion arose as an opposition to the complete disruption of the traditional communal land ownership system of indigenous residents because of the Permanent Settlement introduced in 1793." (Backdrop)

5) A: LAA 1894 — B: Legal history — C: Undervalued compensation
"The Land Acquisition Act, 1894... provided for compensation but was often very meagre. It hardly accounted for loss of livelihood and did not offer any room for dissent from the dispossessed." (LAA genealogy)

6) A: Ancient sources — B: Comparative history — C: Pre-colonial tenure
"The Sanskrit word for land is Vasundhara... land was often considered something which created wealth but was not wealth; state acted as trustee rather than absolute owner." (Ancient)

7) A: Mughal section — B: Institutional comparison — C: Intermediaries
"Mughals relied heavily on intermediaries... jagirdars and zamindars collected revenues and administered local governance." (Mughal)

8) A: British technical tools — B: Legibility (Scott) — C: Cadastral surveys
"Cadastral surveys led to a clear mapping of administrative units... Each landholding was assigned as a parcel, khasra, and was mapped with its owner's name with clearly marked boundaries." (Mapping)

9) A: Colonial law examples — B: Institutional continuity — C: Permanent Settlement
"Permanent Settlement (1793) made zamindars the hereditary owners of large estates on the condition they would pay fixed annual revenues to the British... this strengthened the landlord class." (Permanent Settlement)

10) A: Economics of colonial grabs — B: Extraction rationale — C: Trade strategy
"By mid-19th century, revenues from opium ranked second only to revenues from land in British India's budget." (Opium/finance)

11) A: Consequences — B: Dispossession & migration — C: Indentured labour

12) A: Measurement & mapping — B: Survey technology — C: Great Trigonometrical Survey
"William Lambton initiated the Great Trigonometrical Survey... It took 70 years and created the ‘Great Arc’ and transformed cartography in India." (Survey)

13) A: Legal lineage — B: Acquisition laws — C: Bengal Regulation I (1824)
